Our Work
Our job is to save the surplus and food items that are discarded or even rejected. If you are a restaurant owner, bakery shops owner, hoteliers, food court owners whose food has lost its commercial value such as out of specifications, almost expired, excess stock and close to the date Best Before or Use By but still safe for human use, you can donate it to here for donnes who will make redistribution to people in need from here.

Frequently Asking Questions

Who is Food Liberty?

FOD LIBERTY is a charitable fwill be the foundation where catalyst for the effort to reduce food waste while addressing the issue of increasing cost of living, especially the poor or the B40. We aim to rescue surplus food from the manufacturer, wholesaler, hypermarket and hotel industry; sort and re-distribute it to the identified targeted B40 communities in higher learning institution (IPT), charity home, welfare center and Program Perumahan Rakyat Termiskin (PPRT) through our strategic partners to reduce food waste and to help to ease and lessen the cost of living of the underprivileged Malaysian.



How to join Food Liberty?

Food Liberty is a Web based online platform. To be a food donor, sign up through here. Post a food and wait for the food to be collected. To be a donee or food receiver, sign up the form through here and can book the posted excess food by viewing them under food menu. To join our team and to contribute, email to us at info@foodliberty.my or connect with us through our website.



Who can be a food donor?

Anyone with end of the day unsold food, such as restaurant owners, bakery shops, hoteliers, food courts, buffet restaurants, cafes, mixed rice outlets among others.



Who can be a donee?

Any NGOs and Food Charities, and have food need among other daily essentials.



Do you accept expired food?

Currently NO due to the regulation by Ministry of Health in Malaysia, but we are looking into options to save food under this category. You may contact us if you have a good proposal, we are ready to embark in a working relationship with any organization having the same mindset that is Love Food Hate Waste. In Malaysia, it is an offence to sell food beyond its expiry date in Malaysia. Act 281 Section 24 of the Food Act 1983 prohibits the sale of food “not of the nature, substance and quality demanded” and sale of expired food falls under this. Upon conviction offenders face a stiff jail term up to five years or a fine, or both. Further, the Food Regulations 1985 mandates that all food packaging must bear a date marking. “Expiry date, “Use by”, Consume by” and “Best before” are all taken to mean the same thing, that is, the expiry date.
